1 Abstract

The sites regression (SREG) model puts the genotype main effects into the bilinear terms, so its biplot shows which genotypes do best where. This vignette fits it with the first year as prior, then shows how the weight of the earlier trial changes the posterior and how WAIC chooses the number of terms.

3 The weight of the earlier trial

.n0 sets how many observations per cell the earlier trial counts for. By default it is its number of replicates (3).

w <- c(0.5, 3, 12)
res <-
  data.table::rbindlist(
      l = lapply(
          X   = w
        , FUN = function(n0) {
            f <-
              bayes_ammi(
                  .data       = cultivo2009
                , .y          = y
                , .gen        = entry
                , .env        = site
                , .rep        = rep
                , .nIter      = 1500
                , .prior_data = cultivo2008
                , .n0         = n0
                , .model      = "AMMI"
                , .k          = 2
                , .burnin     = 500
                , .thin       = 1
                , .chains     = 1

                      mu_mean     = 0

                    , tau_mean    = 0
                    , tau_var     = 1e+8

                    , delta_var   = 1e+8

                    , lambda_var  = 1e+8
                    , nu          = 1

As \(n_0\) grows the posterior moves from the second year (grand mean 3627) towards the first (4069). With \(n_0 = 3\) it sits halfway. A small \(n_0\) is the choice when the earlier trial was grown under different conditions.

4 How many terms?

WAIC compares fits with different numbers of terms (Watanabe 2010; Vehtari et al. 2017):

fits <-
  lapply(
      X   = c(1, 2, 3, 5)
    , FUN = function(K) {
        bayes_ammi(
            .data       = cultivo2009
          , .y          = y
          , .gen        = entry
          , .env        = site
          , .rep        = rep
          , .nIter      = 1500
          , .prior_data = cultivo2008
          , .n0         = NULL
          , .model      = "AMMI"
          , .k          = K
          , .burnin     = 500
          , .thin       = 1
          , .chains     = 1
          , .priors     = bys_priors(
                mu_mean     = 0
              , mu_var      = 1e+8
              , tau_mean    = 0
              , tau_var     = 1e+8
              , delta_mean  = 0
              , delta_var   = 1e+8
              , lambda_mean = 0
              , lambda_var  = 1e+8
              , nu          = 1
              , s2          = NULL
              )
          , .level      = 0.95
          , .seed       = 5
          , .align      = TRUE
          , .waic       = TRUE
          )
      }
    )
names(fits) <- paste0("K", c(1, 2, 3, 5))

WAIC prefers K1; K2 lie within two standard errors of the difference (dWAIC_se), so the data do not separate them. With the first year as a strong prior, the singular values of every term are pulled towards that year’s, and few terms suffice for prediction. The paper chose three terms with the Bayes factor, which compares the marginal likelihoods of the models rather than their predictive accuracy.
